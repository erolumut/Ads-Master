# Research Dossier: Marketing Compliance (Claims and Policy Gate)

> Research sweep: 2026-10-08. Scope: advertising claims law by vertical and market (EU, Germany, Netherlands, UK, US, Turkey, MENA basics), platform ad and store policies, consumer and pricing law, reviews and influencer disclosure, environmental claims, AI disclosure, privacy in marketing and accessibility basics. Method: 48 web searches focused on 2025 to 2026 changes, cross checked across official pages and law firm summaries; primary pages could not be fetched directly from the research environment, so official texts were verified through at least one summary of the primary source, and canonical legal texts not re-checked are labelled as such. Evidence labels follow docs/AUTHORING_SPEC.md section 7. This dossier is research for the compliance skill, not legal advice.

## Executive summary

1. **The EU green claims regime changed on 2026-09-27.** Directive (EU) 2024/825 now bans generic environmental claims without recognised top environmental performance, offset based product neutrality claims ("climate neutral"), and self made sustainability labels; Germany (UWG amendment promulgated 2026-02-19) and the Netherlands (Senate 2026-05-26) transposed on time, 20 member states got formal notices on 2026-05-28. The separate Green Claims Directive is blocked, not formally withdrawn [Contested]. [Official, 2026]
2. **Turkey rewrote its advertising regulation from 2026-08-01** (RG 33297, 2026-07-01): discount reference price is now the lowest price in the last 10 days per channel (was 30), influencer posts must say "reklam" or "tanıtım" on the first screen, AI use that significantly affects consumer decisions must be disclosed, AI replicas of real people endorsing products are banned, profiling based targeted ads to children are banned, green claim principles became binding. [Official via law firm summaries, 2026-07]
3. **EU AI Act Article 50 applies since 2026-08-02**: deep fakes in ads must be disclosed in a perceivable way; Commission guidelines C(2026) 5054 (2026-07-20) and a voluntary code of practice (2026-06-10, about 190 signatories) shape practice; only the machine readable marking duty for pre-existing systems got a grace period to 2026-12-02 (Reg 2026/1744). [Official, 2026]
4. **Fake reviews moved from rule to enforcement on both sides of the Atlantic.** FTC warning letters (2025-12-22), the first money resolution under 16 CFR 465 (TruHeight, USD 750,000, 2026-07), a DOJ case (2026-05); the CMA opened five DMCC fake review investigations (2026-03-27). [Official, 2025 to 2026]
5. **UK DMCC enforcement is real and pricing led**: four CMA penalties to 2026-10 (about GBP 6.3 million in total), led by AA Driving School's GBP 4.2 million drip pricing fine (2026-04); urgency and fake review cases are pending; the subscription regime is now targeted for January 2027. [Official via secondary, 2026]
6. **US subscription law runs on ROSCA and the states, not on click to cancel.** The FTC rule was vacated (2025-07-08); a new ANPRM (2026-03-13) has not produced a proposed rule; Amazon paid USD 2.5 billion (2025-09); California, New York, NYC, Minnesota, Colorado, Maryland, Connecticut and Louisiana tightened auto renewal rules in 2025 to 2026. [Official, 2025 to 2026]
7. **Health advertising is the hottest enforcement area**: FDA's DTC crackdown (2025-09-09) and planned removal of "adequate provision" (NPRM targeted 2026-12), waves of compounded GLP-1 telehealth warning letters (2025-09, 2026-03, 2026-06), and the ASA's weight loss POM campaign (99% compliance by 2026-01, first oral weight loss medicine ad bans in 2026-10). [Official, 2025 to 2026]
8. **Platforms rewrote category rules all year.** Meta revised Health and Wellness (2026-07-22, before and after for cosmetic procedures to adults); Google added EEA financial services verification (enforcement from 2026-07-23), stricter gambling account health (2026-09-14) and a unified alcohol country table (2026-09-30); TikTok logged a dozen category updates; Microsoft restructured healthcare (2026-07-01); OpenAI's ChatGPT ads keep most regulated categories out (v1.6, 2026-09-10). [Official, 2026]
9. **Finance and crypto have hard dates**: MiCA transition ended EU wide 2026-07-01; CCD2 credit ad rules apply 2026-11-20 ("Borrowing money costs money" warning) although most states transposed late; Turkey blocks unlicensed crypto platforms under Law 7518 and the first SPK permits went only to bank custodians. [Official, 2026]
10. **Consent rules moved in both directions**: the TCPA one-to-one consent rule died (2025-01-24) and the FCC narrowed revocation (2026-09-30), while UK PECR fines rose to GDPR levels (2026-02-05), Turkey's KVKK invalidated bundled SMS code consents (2025-06), and US state health data laws (Washington MHMDA, Maryland MODPA) restrict health audiences. [Official, 2025 to 2026]

## Best practice consensus

- A claims registry with exact approved wording, legal basis and conditions is the backbone of fast compliance: reviewers approve once and reuse. [Practitioner consensus]
- Evidence first: substantiate before publication, keep the file, and give every fact an owner and an expiry. The EU, UK (CAP 3.7), US (FTC substantiation policy) and Turkey (burden of proof on the advertiser) align on this. [Official]
- Review the net impression: images, product names, badges and the landing page are part of the claim. [Official: UCPD average consumer; FTC net impression]
- Health claims on foods and supplements use the register wording and conditions; no disease language. [Official]
- Price claims come from price history exports, not from memory or list prices. [Official: Art 6a, C-330/23, TR Art 14]
- Disclose material connections in the market's words, upfront, plus the platform tool; monitor creators after posting. [Official: FTC, ASA, SRC, Reklam Kurulu]
- Treat platform policy and law as two separate gates. [Practitioner consensus]
- Escalate regulated categories and comparative claims to counsel by default. [Practitioner consensus]
- Sweep live and legacy content when a rule starts; old pages and posts still count. [Practitioner consensus; ACM and German commentary on EmpCo]

## What top operators do differently

1. They publish a claim menu per product before creative starts, so briefs are written with approved wording and reviews become confirmations.
2. They run a pattern screen in the content pipeline (CI or CMS hook) and fail builds only on BLOCK, keeping humans for judgment.
3. They store evidence like code: versioned, linked to fact IDs, with owners and expiries, and an alert 30 days before expiry.
4. They compute nutrition claim eligibility and price reduction references automatically from the product and price databases.
5. They brief creators with a market label table and contract clauses, then audit a sample of live posts weekly.
6. They plan for dated rule changes 60 to 90 days ahead (EmpCo, Turkey 2026, CCD2, AI Act) and sweep legacy content.
7. They separate law from platform policy and keep a rejection log that feeds the registry.
8. They track over-blocking with the business: every blocked high value claim gets a path to evidence (study, certification) instead of a permanent no.
9. In regulated categories they keep licence numbers and permissions as facts with expiries and check them before every launch.
10. They never use AI people as customers or experts and build synthetic performer disclosures to the strictest standard (California 2027).
