# Research Dossier: Offer Strategy, Pricing and Merchandising

> Research date: 2026-10-08. Scope: offer design and economics (discounts, gifts, free shipping, thresholds, bundles, price ladders, guarantees, subscriptions, lead gen and SaaS offers), pricing research and price tests, promo calendars and sales events (global, US, Turkey, Netherlands), channel conflict (retail, marketplaces, MAP and resale price maintenance), price display law handoffs, offer measurement and platform implementation.
>
> Method and limits: 27 web searches (extended mode for 2025 to 2026 facts and niche topics, standard mode for established research). Direct page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked by DNS in the research environment, so 2025 to 2026 findings rely on the content of search results from the listed pages (official pages where they surfaced, law firm alerts, vendor and press coverage), not full-page reads. Classic academic and legal sources are cited from prior knowledge and marked "prior knowledge". Every claim carries an evidence label; anything single sourced or not confirmable is marked [Unverified]. Re-verify starred legal dates before external use.

## 1. Executive summary

1. Pricing law tightened on three fronts in 2025 to 2026: reference prices (EU 30 day rule enforced by ACM and others; Turkey cut its lookback from 30 to 10 days for price tags on 2025-10-11 and for discount advertising from 2026-08-01), drip pricing (UK blacklist since 2025-04-06 with CMA fines from 2025; US all-in price laws in California, Minnesota, Virginia, Massachusetts, Colorado and Connecticut) and personalized pricing (New York disclosure law since 2025-11-10; Maryland, Connecticut and New Jersey surveillance pricing laws in 2026) [Official and law firm sources, 2025 to 2026]. Offers must be designed for these rules, not checked afterwards.
2. Subscriptions remain an enforcement priority even without a federal click to cancel rule: the Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC rule on 2025-07-08, Amazon settled the Prime case for up to USD 2.5B in September 2025, the FTC restarted rulemaking with an ANPRM on 2026-03-13 and kept suing under ROSCA (JustAnswer, Hims and Hers), and New York City's click to cancel rule took effect in October 2026 [Official, 2025 to 2026]. The UK subscription regime slipped to spring 2027 [Official, 2026-04].
3. Shopify's offer stack changed under merchants' feet: the unified Discount Function API (2025-04) replaced separate product, order and shipping functions, and Shopify Scripts were switched off on 2026-06-30 [Official, 2025 to 2026]. Expect silent discount failures and double discounting in Plus store audits through Q4 2026.
4. Deep acquisition discounts are the most expensive habit in DTC: Lewis (2006) found a 35% acquisition discount produced customers worth about half as much over the long run [Study, 2006]. Acquisition investment (media plus shipping subsidy plus bonus product cost plus incremental discount cost) must sit next to media CPA in every report.
5. Free shipping is powerful and double-edged: extra costs remain the top fixable cart abandonment reason (39% of abandoners in recent Baymard data, 48% in February 2024), but free shipping promotions raise returns and can be unprofitable net (Shehu, Papies and Neslin 2020), while threshold policies raised orders without a returns penalty in a 26 million order EU dataset [Study, 2020 to 2025]. Thresholds need gap fillers and a returns guardrail.
6. BNPL lifts sales for some segments (about 20% merchant sales lift in Berg et al., JFE 2025; USD 1.03B of US Cyber Monday 2025 online spend per Adobe) and is now regulated in the UK (from 2026-07-15) and, from 2026-11-20, in the EU under CCD2 [Study and Official, 2025 to 2026]. Treat it as a high ticket offer lever with legal wording.
7. Classic pricing psychology needs evidence labels: anchoring replicates well (Many Labs 1), but decoy effects largely vanish with realistic stimuli and a 140,000 session field test found no general attraction effect; choice overload averages near zero in meta-analysis [Study, 1982 to 2025]. Design tiers for real customer needs and test.
8. Synthetic respondents are not a pricing method yet: a 2026 Journal of Marketing Analytics benchmark against incentive compatible WTP found synthetic WTP too high for hedonic products and inconsistent for utilitarian products [Study, 2026]. Use them for questionnaire drafts only.
9. Holiday discounting is stable but deep: Adobe forecasts USD 275.1B US online holiday spend in 2026 with Cyber Week discounts up to 30% off list, after USD 257.8B in 2025 [Study, Adobe 2026-09]. Category specific depth and reference price planning matter more than matching every promo.
10. Competition authorities keep fining resale price maintenance, including discount caps and online price monitoring: EU fines on Gucci, Chloé and Loewe (EUR 157.4M, 2025-10-14), Italy's Morellato (over EUR 25M, 2026-03-17), and repeated Turkish Competition Board decisions [Official, 2025 to 2026]. DTC exclusive assortments and value-adds are the safe answer to channel conflict.

## 2. State of the domain in 2026 (with numbers)

### 2.1 Demand and discount depth
- Adobe 2026 forecast (released 2026-09-28): US online holiday season USD 275.1B (up 6.7%); Cyber Week USD 47.5B (up 7.4%, 17.3% of season); Cyber Monday (2026-11-30) USD 15.1B; Black Friday USD 12.9B (up 9.2%); October USD 95.8B; discounts up to 30% off list in Cyber Week, up to 14% in early November, up to 21% before Thanksgiving, 10% to 15% through December; category peaks: electronics 30%, toys 29%, TVs 23%, computers 23%, apparel 23%, sporting goods 19%, appliances 18%, furniture 18% [Study, Adobe 2026-09].
- Adobe 2025 actuals: season USD 257.8B (up 6.8%, above its USD 253.4B forecast); Cyber Monday USD 14.25B (up 7.1%); Black Friday USD 11.8B (up 9.1%); Cyber Week USD 44.2B; mobile 57.5% of Cyber Monday sales; BNPL USD 1.03B on Cyber Monday; Cyber Monday peak discounts electronics 31%, toys 28%, apparel 25%, computers 23%, TVs 22%, furniture and appliances 19%, sporting goods 17% [Study, Adobe 2025-12]. Adobe's year over year discount baselines differ between releases (for example electronics 30.1% vs 31%), so compare within one release.
- Prime Big Deal Days ran 2026-10-06 to 07 with an Adobe forecast of USD 9.9B; a secondary source reports USD 9.86B actual [Unverified].

### 2.2 Cost side pressures on offers
- US de minimis exemption suspended for all countries from 2025-08-29 (China and Hong Kong from 2025-05-02); secondary sources report a 75% drop in sub-threshold shipments and per parcel duties and fees that hit low AOV orders hardest [Official and secondary, 2025 to 2026].
- EU temporary EUR 3 customs duty per item type on low value parcels (up to EUR 150) from 2026-07-01, mainly affecting non-EU sellers; a separate EUR 2 handling fee per consignment was reported for November 2026 [Official, 2026-06; handling fee Unverified].
- Implication: landed cost and shipping inputs from 2025 are stale for cross-border sellers; offers modeled on them may now lose money.

### 2.3 Pricing law and enforcement
- EU: prior price rule (Omnibus, since 2022-05-28); CJEU C-330/23 (2024-09-26) requires percentages to be based on the 30 day prior price [Official, prior knowledge]. Digital Fairness Act proposal planned for Q4 2026, not tabled as of mid-September 2026; consultation (closed 2025-10-24) asked about an outright drip pricing ban, unfair personalization and subscription traps [Official plan, 2026].
- Netherlands: ACM fined five webshops EUR 621,000 in June 2024 (Leen Bakker EUR 130,000 and Koopjedeal EUR 163,000 upheld by the court on 2025-11-11); a pre Black Friday 2025 check found misleading price claims at 18 of 24 sellers; maximum EUR 900,000 per violation [Official, 2024 to 2025].
- Turkey: price tag regulation lookback cut from 30 to 10 days (Official Gazette 33044, 2025-10-11); advertising regulation amended (Official Gazette 33297, 2026-07-01) so discount ads use the lowest price of the prior 10 days in the same channel from 2026-08-01, including loyalty and conditional offers [Official, via secondary sources].
- UK: DMCC unfair practices regime from 2025-04-06 with drip pricing on the blacklist; CMA price transparency guidance (CMA209) on 2025-11-18; fines against AA and BSM (GBP 4.2M plus refunds), StubHub (2026-06-23), Marks Electrical (GBP 720,000); new probes into Trainline, Virgin Atlantic and RED Driving School on 2026-08-19; Price Marking Order reforms from 2026-04-06 (wider unit pricing scope, standard units, ordinary price next to loyalty price) [Official and law firm sources, 2025 to 2026].
- US: state all-in price laws (California 2024-07-01, Minnesota 2025-01-01, Virginia 2025-07-01, Massachusetts 2025-09-02, Colorado 2026-01-01, Connecticut 2026-07-01; Illinois pending for 2027); FTC fees rule limited to live events and short term lodging [law firm sources, 2025 to 2026].
- Personalized pricing: New York Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act in effect 2025-11-10 (survived a First Amendment challenge; appeal pending); New York AG letter to Instacart on 2026-01-08 after a study found up to 23% price differences for identical items; One Fair Price Act passed the legislature in June 2026 and awaited signature; Maryland (food retailers, 2026-10-01), Connecticut and New Jersey (signed 2026-07-23, most provisions 2027-08-01, private right of action) enacted restrictions [Official and law firm sources, 2026].

### 2.4 Subscriptions
- FTC click to cancel vacated (2025-07-08), ANPRM published 2026-03-13; ROSCA enforcement continues with penalties up to USD 53,088 per violation; Amazon Prime settlement up to USD 2.5B (USD 1B civil penalty, USD 1.5B refunds) with a decline button and cancellation as easy as enrollment; Hims and Hers complaint (2026-07-29) treats non-financial terms as material [Official, 2025 to 2026].
- UK DMCC subscription regime delayed to spring 2027 (government statement 2026-04-02) [Official, 2026-04].
- Subscription tooling consolidates: Recharge reported to be joining with Skio (May 2026) [Unverified].

### 2.5 Platforms
- Shopify: unified Discount Function API (changelog 2025-05-21); limit of 25 active automatic discounts including app discounts and 25 discount functions; up to 5 product or order codes plus 1 shipping code per order; Scripts sunset 2026-06-30 with Script Editor access to 2026-07-30; Bundles app covers fixed bundles and multipacks, mix and match needs Cart Transform apps; Rollouts (Winter '26, expanded June 2026) tests themes and checkout configurations but, per secondary reviews, not prices [Official and secondary, 2025 to 2026].
- SaaS billing: Stripe completed its Metronome acquisition on 2026-01-14 [Unverified, secondary].

### 2.6 SaaS pricing models
- Growth Unhinged 2026 survey (230 companies, April to May 2026): hybrid pricing 37% (from 25% a year earlier on that measure); investors favor hybrid 35%, outcome based 26%, usage 24%, flat fee 10%, seats 5% [Study, 2026].
- ChartMogul and ProductLed (200 B2B products): median free to paid 8%; free trial 57% of products, freemium 26%, reverse trial 7%; reverse trial good 4% to 6%, great 8% to 12% [Study, 2025].
- First Page Sage (86 companies, data to late 2025): opt-out trial to paid about 49%, opt-in about 18%, freemium organic signup to paid about 2.6% [Study, 2025].

## 4. Best practice consensus

| Practice | Why | Evidence |
|----------|-----|----------|
| Model every offer in contribution per order and acquisition investment before launch | Conversion gains can hide contribution losses | [Practitioner consensus]; METRICS.md definition |
| Remove non-price barriers (shipping cost visibility, risk, effort, payment) before discounting | Cheaper per incremental order | Baymard abandonment data [Study, 2024 to 2025]; return leniency meta-analysis [Study, 2016] |
| Cap and cohort first order discounts | Deep acquisition discounts attract lower value customers | Lewis 2006 [Study] |
| Use thresholds with gap fillers and a returns guardrail | Thresholds raise AOV; padding and returns erode gains | Groningen report, Shehu et al., UMD and IIMA papers [Study] |
| Prefer value-adds and exclusive bundles where channels compete | Avoids featured offer loss and retailer conflict; avoids RPM temptation | Amazon featured offer policy [Official forum]; RPM fines [Official] |
| Keep price history and plan promos around reference price windows | Legal requirement and reference price health | Omnibus, ACM, Turkey rules [Official] |
| Measure promos with baselines, holdouts and pull-forward | Most promo volume is borrowed or subsidized | [Practitioner consensus] |
| Disclose subscription terms and make cancellation easy | Enforcement priority everywhere | FTC, Amazon order, state laws [Official] |
| Treat lab pricing effects as hypotheses | Several do not survive realistic settings | Decoy and choice overload literature [Study] |

## 5. Contested topics (both sides)

| Topic | Side A | Side B | Working position |
|-------|--------|--------|------------------|
| Decoy pricing on tier pages | Classic attraction effect (Huber, Payne, Puto 1982) and many lab replications; widely used on pricing pages | Frederick et al. and Yang and Lynn 2014 failed to replicate with realistic stimuli; field test with over 140,000 sessions found no general effect | Add a tier only if customers genuinely want it; test |
| Choice overload | Jam study (2000) shows fewer purchases with more options | Meta-analysis mean effect near zero (2010); effect only under specific moderators (2015) | 2 to 4 rungs, clear default, test |
| Free shipping thresholds | Raise orders and sales without a returns penalty (Groningen, 26.21M orders) | Free shipping promotions raise returns and can be unprofitable (Shehu et al. 2020); padding with intent to return (UMD, IIMA) | Test with returns as guardrail; return frictions for padded baskets |
| BNPL | Raises merchant sales about 20%, lets merchants price discriminate (Berg et al. 2025) | Overspending and harm for vulnerable users (Central Bank of Ireland 2023 and 2025; Di Maggio et al. "liquidity flypaper") | Offer on high ticket items, responsible messaging, regulated wording |
| Synthetic respondents for pricing | Vendors claim up to 90% alignment | 2026 benchmark found systematic WTP errors; diagnostics paper shows hidden subgroup errors | Exploration only, calibrate with humans, decide with live tests |
| Visitor level price A/B tests | Fastest causal answer; tools exist | Trust and legal exposure (Instacart case, NY disclosure law, EU personalization disclosure), feed mismatch | Prefer discount, new SKU, geo or time split designs |
| Reverse trials vs free trials | Reverse trials combine trial urgency with a freemium safety net | ChartMogul sample small and differences not significant | Fit to product: needs a useful free tier |
| Per-seat pricing decline | Hybrid and usage models rising (Growth Unhinged 2026) | Over 80% still use seats as one component (CRV) | Hybrid with seats plus credits for AI usage |
| Recommended retail price as reference ("adviesprijs") | European Commission guidance leaves room for a struck RRP with explanation | ACM says a struck reference price must be the 30 day low | In the Netherlands follow ACM; elsewhere get compliance sign off |

## 6. What top operators do differently
- They maintain a living cost stack per SKU (landed cost with duties, carrier surcharges, fees by payment method, returns by category) and recompute offers when inputs move.
- They keep a promo days budget and discount rate target, and alternate price events with value-add events.
- They cohort by acquisition offer and kill offers that win cheap but non-repeating customers.
- They own price history as compliance evidence and plan calendars backwards from reference price windows.
- They build DTC exclusive kits, sizes and colorways so promotions do not create like-for-like comparisons with partners.
- They run holdouts on promo sends and geo or switchback tests for sitewide offers, and always read the 2 to 4 weeks after.
- They hand lifecycle flows hard discount caps per segment so automation never invents discounts.
- They specify discounts with test carts and rollback before anything goes live, and audit stacking after platform changes (Scripts sunset).

## 7. Common expensive mistakes
- Reporting media CPA without the first order discount, shipping subsidy and gift costs.
- Sitewide discounts that mostly subsidize buyers who would have purchased anyway, judged on revenue.
- Unintended stacking, especially after migrations (Scripts to Functions) or with old automatic discounts left on.
- Using recommended retail prices or pre-raised prices as "was" prices (ACM fines; Omnibus; Turkey 10 day rule).
- Free shipping on small orders that turns every first order into a loss; thresholds without gap fillers.
- Deep multipack discounts that reduce future purchases.
- DTC promos that suppress the Amazon featured offer or provoke retail partners; then trying to make resellers raise prices (RPM).
- Subscription first order incentives that attract churners; cancellation flows with multiple save steps.
- Price tests that create feed price mismatches and Merchant Center disapprovals.
- Ignoring landed cost changes from tariffs, de minimis changes and the EU parcel duty.
