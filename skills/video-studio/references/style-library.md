# Style Library

> Purpose: 36 ad video styles with their hook, proof and CTA beats, when to use them, the production mode and the risks. Pick a style at P3 (treatment). A concept can be executed in several styles; each style change is a new visual world for the variety ledger. The kit idea of "one story, many themes" applies: the same message told in different styles is how you build creative diversity without changing the strategy.

Legend for Mode: F = real footage (phone, creator, studio), C = code-driven motion (HyperFrames or Remotion), G = generative video or images, A = AI avatar or synthetic voice, H = hybrid. Risk tags: [claims], [policy], [rights], [disclosure], [trust].

## Style selection decision tree

```
1. What proof does the concept rely on?
   real customer experience  -> S01, S10, S05, S07 (real people only)
   product does something visible -> S11, S13, S14, S18, S16 (where allowed), S12
   a number, a mechanism or a workflow -> S26, S27, S19, S04, S25
   an offer -> S24, S21, S29
   identity or emotion -> S03, S20, S31, S33, S30
2. Which placements carry the budget?
   TikTok, Reels, Shorts -> prefer Family A and B, native look
   Meta Feed, LinkedIn   -> prefer readable text styles (C) or demos with strong captions
   YouTube in-stream, CTV -> S03, S04, S11, S19, S26 with brand early
3. What can be produced at this tier this week?
   Starter -> S01 (founder or customer on phone), S11, S13, S21, S24
   Growth  -> add creators (S02, S07), code templates (S22, S23, S25, S27)
   Scale   -> full library, templates for C, creator roster for A, generative worlds S35 for diversity
4. Check the variety ledger: never the same style_id as the last 2 launches in the same ad set unless it is an iteration.
```

## Tone settings per style (starting points)

| Tone | Pacing | Type | Transitions | Sound | Fits styles |
|------|--------|------|-------------|-------|-------------|
| Urgent | 0.5 to 1.2 s shots | Condensed, uppercase, big | Hard cuts, whip pans, beat cut with hold frame | Ticks, risers, hard stops | S24, S21, S15 |
| Trustworthy | Even 2 to 3 s | Clear sans, numbers in mono | Match cut (shape), cursor carry | Warm VO, light music | S04, S26, S27, S10 |
| Playful | Syncopated | Rounded, stickers | Object carry, paper fold, jump cuts | Pops, plucks | S30, S32, S34, S12 |
| Premium | Slow, long holds | Serif display, wide tracking | Push through, focal iris, rack focus | Low drone, single note, silence | S13, S35, S09 |
| Native and raw | Jump cuts, no polish | Platform native text | Jump cuts only | Natural voice, room sound | S01, S02, S05, S07, S33 |
| Technical | Grid locked | Mono, tabular numbers | Split stack, cursor carry | Clicks, data blips | S27, S26, S19 |

Tone may shift once inside an ad (urgent problem, then trustworthy proof, then warm CTA). Record tone per beat in the storyboard.

## Transition kit for performance ads

Narrative transitions (from the saas-motion-kit atlas, reduced to what earns its place in a 15 to 30 s ad). Spend narrative transitions where the story turns: hook to problem, problem to product, proof to CTA. Hard cuts carry everything else.

| Transition | Meaning | Use at | Avoid |
|------------|---------|--------|-------|
| Hard cut on action | Continuity, pace | Everywhere | Cutting on dead air |
| Match cut (shape) | Same thing, new meaning (problem becomes solution) | Hook to reveal | Near miss in position |
| Match cut (motion) | Cause then effect | Workflow steps | Cutting on a still object |
| Object carry | One item moves through the system | Product into use | Twice in a row |
| Cursor carry | Click here and this opens | UI demos | More than 3 clicks per ad |
| Whip pan | Meanwhile, or before and after | Comparison | More than twice |
| Push through | Going inside the product | Reveal | Right after a zoom |
| Product shape mask | Brand to product | First reveal | With a text mask in the same ad |
| Beat cut with hold frame | Wait for it | Before the offer | Off the beat |
| Speed ramp | Energy, time passing | Demos (label "sped up" if it changes a performance claim) | Faking product speed |
| Freeze and rewind | How did we get here | Result first hooks | Premium brands with VHS noise |

## Reference breakdowns (borrow grammar, never assets)

When `creative-strategy` or the human brings reference ads (competitor or category ads from Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center, YouTube):
1. Record a credit row: id, creator or brand, title, link, date seen, what we borrow (rhythm, structure, transition type, camera idea).
2. Measure grammar: cuts per 10 s, first cut time, hook type, text density, length, where the offer appears.
3. Borrow only grammar. Never their footage, frames, music, characters, logos, copy or a look so close it passes as theirs.
4. Mark borrowed shots `ref:<id>` in the storyboard notes. Keep reference files out of the client repo and never re-upload them.
5. Mix 2 to 3 references; copying one ad produces a lookalike that the delivery system may group with it and that invites legal risk.
