# Truth, Disclosure and Rights

> Purpose: make every frame honest, every disclosure correct and every asset licensed. This is practical guidance, not legal advice. `compliance` owns the claims registry and the final policy call; the human or counsel decides anything uncertain. Verify dates and texts at the sources in the Freshness Protocol before relying on them.

## 1. The truth rule: clean or imaginary, never fake

Adapted from the saas-motion-kit components rule and extended to physical products and people.

| Subject | Use the real thing when | Use a truthful imaginary or illustrative version when | Never |
|---------|-------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|-------|
| Software UI | The real UI is current, calm, on brand and shows the proof moment (score 7 to 8 of 8) | The real UI is cluttered, legacy or not designed yet: a simplified component that shows one true capability, in brand tokens, with demo data labeled as example | Show a feature that does not exist; invent customer names, logos, usage numbers or results |
| Physical product | Always for the product itself: real footage or real photography, composited into any generated scene | Mechanism illustrations (how a filter traps particles) labeled "Illustration" | Generate or alter the product's shape, color, shade, size, label, packaging or texture; show a performance it does not achieve |
| Results | Real, documented results with typicality disclosure where required | Clearly labeled dramatization of the problem (not of the result) | AI-made "after" images, retouched results, sped-up results presented as real time |
| People | Real customers, experts and staff with releases, saying what they actually experienced | Labeled actors or AI presenters acting out use, never vouching | AI or actor "customers" giving testimonials; synthetic experts; real people's likeness without consent |
| Data | Sourced numbers with the source and date on screen or in the caption | Demo scale numbers inside imaginary UI, visibly example data | Statistics presented as facts without substantiation ("used by 10,000 teams" without proof) |
| Places and events | Real locations with permission | Generated settings that do not claim to be a real place or event | Fake news, fake events, fake reviews, fake social posts, fake system dialogs |

Demonstrations: a demonstration offered as proof of a claim must show what it appears to show. Mock-ups that stand in for the real test are deceptive when they are presented as proof (US Supreme Court, FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive, 1965, the "Rapid Shave" sandpaper case) [Official legal precedent]. If a shot is sped up, re-staged or enhanced in a way that affects the claim, label it on screen ("Sped up 2x", "Dramatization") or do not use it.

Beauty and cosmetics: filters or AI retouching that exaggerate a product's effect mislead (the UK ASA has ruled against filtered beauty ads) [Official, ASA rulings]. Show unretouched results or none.

## 2. Testimonials, reviews and endorsements

| Rule | Source |
|------|--------|
| Fake reviews and testimonials are banned, including those attributed to people who do not exist (for example AI generated) or who did not have the experience; civil penalties up to 53,088 USD per violation | FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465, effective 2024-10-21 [Official]; the FTC sent warning letters to about 10 companies on 2025-12-22 [Official via secondary] |
| Endorsements reflect honest opinions and actual experience; material connections (payment, free product, employment) disclosed clearly in the video itself (on screen and ideally spoken) | FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023 [Official] |
| Results not typical must be qualified with generally expected results, and substantiated either way | FTC Endorsement Guides [Official] |
| Virtual influencers and AI avatars are covered as endorsers if they appear to be people | FTC 2023 revision; commentators 2026 [Practitioner consensus] |
| UK: fake reviews banned under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 | [Official, verify commencement] |
| EU: hidden advertising and misleading actions banned (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive) | [Official] |

Video implications: real reviews only, verbatim, with source and date (review card template T5); creator videos carry "Paid partnership" or "#ad" in the first seconds and the platform partnership label; customer clips need a signed release and must be typical or qualified.

## 3. AI actors and synthetic performers

Generated people are actors, never customers (saas-motion-kit human layer rule). They may act out using the product; they may never vouch for it, carry a customer name with a quote, or appear in a "customer story".

Disclosure requirements (details and wording in [Avatars, voice and music](avatars-voice-and-music.md) section 3):

| Where | Trigger | Requirement |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| New York (from 2026-06-09) | Ad includes a synthetic performer (non-identifiable human-like asset) and the producer knows | Conspicuous disclosure |
| Hawaii (enacted 2026-07) | Nearly identical to New York [Contested status in some alerts] | Same |
| California (from 2027-01-01) | Synthetic performer prominently featured, including audio-only ads | Clear and conspicuous disclosure near the performer, wording like "this performance features a synthetic digital performer" |
| EU (from 2026-08-02) | Deep fake: realistic AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video of people, objects, places or events that could appear genuine, including a photorealistic invented person | Visible label at first exposure; commercial ads get the standard label |
| US federal | Pending: a 2025-12 executive order targets conflicting state AI laws; no court has suspended these laws as of 2026-10 [Unverified status] | Comply with state laws anyway |

Also label "Actor portrayal" or "Dramatization" when human actors portray customers or situations presented as real.

## 4. EU AI Act Article 50 for ad video

| Point | Detail | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Application date | 2026-08-02 for Article 50 transparency duties | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 [Official] |
| Digital Omnibus | Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (in force 2026-07-27) kept Article 50 dates; gives providers of generative systems already on the market a grace period for machine-readable marking (Article 50(2)) to 2026-12-02 | [Official via law firm summaries, 2026-07] |
| Code of practice | Final code of practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content published 2026-06-10 (voluntary; provider section on marking and detection, deployer section on labelling; at least two machine-readable marking layers; EU icon set for deployers) | European Commission digital strategy site [Official via secondary] |
| Final guidelines | Commission guidelines on Article 50, C(2026) 5054 final, adopted 2026-07-20 | [Official via Bird and Bird, Reed Smith, Faegre Drinker summaries] |
| Who is the deployer | The advertiser or agency that uses the generator, not the model vendor; a vendor watermark does not discharge the deployer's duty | [Law firm summaries, 2026-07] |
| What counts | Photorealistic invented persons; AI product images that could mislead about appearance, features or use; AI-altered footage of real people, places or events | [Law firm summaries of the guidelines, 2026-07] |
| Artistic exception | Narrow; every advertising example in the guidelines gets the standard label | [Same] |
| Label | Perceivable without tools, at first exposure at the latest, visible long enough, inside the content where feasible | [Commission FAQ via secondary] |
| Scope | Publicly accessible content can be in scope even if not aimed at the EU | [Commentary, 2026] |
| Timing | Content generated before 2026-08-02 needs no retroactive label | [Commentary, 2026] |
| Penalties | Up to 15M EUR or 3 percent of worldwide annual turnover for transparency violations | Article 99 [Official] |

Practical rule for EU-facing ads: any realistic generated or manipulated person, product depiction, place or event gets an on-screen label from its first appearance in the ad's language ("Created with AI", "AI-generated presenter", or the EU icon), and the platform AI label setting on. Abstract, clearly stylized graphics and code-driven motion design with no realistic depiction are generally outside the deep fake definition; when unsure, label and ask `compliance`.

Other markets: China's measures for labeling AI-generated synthetic content in force since 2025-09-01 (explicit and implicit labels) [Official]; check the local regulator for Turkey, UK (ASA, CAP Code), Australia, Canada, India and others before launching AI-heavy creative.

## 5. Platform AI labels

| Platform | What happens | Advertiser action |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Meta | "AI info" labels on ads made or significantly edited with Meta's generative tools (2025-02); from 2026-06-01 also on media detected as made with third-party AI tools via signals such as C2PA [Official, Meta Help Center; some blogs date it 2026-07, Contested] | Keep provenance metadata; expect labels; do not strip C2PA |
| TikTok | Realistic AI-generated or significantly edited ad content needs the AIGC label or a clear on-screen disclaimer, or the ad is rejected or restricted; C2PA content auto labeled; Symphony outputs carry labels and watermarks [Official ad policy, 2026; effective date contested] | AIGC label on for `aiG` and `aiP` files; on-screen disclosure too |
| Google and YouTube ads | 2026-07 update: advertisers may add text or visual AI labels inside creatives without breaching overlay policies; an AI label setting rolled out across Google Ads, DV360, CM360, Merchant Center and Ads Editor; Google may auto label assets made with its own AI; election ads keep the mandatory altered or synthetic content disclosure [Official, support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/17257106] | Use the AI label setting and in-creative label where the market requires disclosure (EU, New York, California) |
| YouTube organic | Creators disclose realistic altered or synthetic content | Applies to organic uploads on the channel |
| LinkedIn | Shows C2PA Content Credentials on some AI images [Verify] | Keep provenance |
| Political and social issue ads (Meta, Google) | Mandatory disclosure of digitally created or altered realistic content | Route to the human; TikTok does not allow political ads |

## 6. Rights checklist (assets)

| Asset | Requirement | Where to record |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| Music | License covers paid ads, territories, channels and term (see [Avatars, voice and music](avatars-voice-and-music.md)) | Music license log, registry `music_license` |
| Voice talent | Buyout covering media, term, territory; union terms where relevant | Talent release ID |
| On-camera people | Signed release (customers, staff, street interviews, creators) including paid ads and AI use if any | `talent_release_ids` |
| Digital twins and voice clones | Separate explicit consent for AI generation, script approval, revocation | Release ID plus platform consent record |
| Creator content | Paid usage rights, duration, territories, whitelisting or Spark authorization in writing | Creator registry (creative-strategy) |
| Stock footage, images, SFX | License covers paid advertising in the target territories | Asset log |
| Fonts | Desktop and web or app license covers video and ads | Brand asset log |
| Logos and trademarks | Official files only; never redraw a logo; competitor marks only with legal approval | Brand asset log |
| Third-party content on screen (reviews, articles, Reddit posts, app store screens) | Permission or legal review; accurate and current | Notes per shot |
| Memes, characters, artworks | Often copyrighted; assume not licensed | Legal review |
| Reference ads | Credit, borrow grammar only, never assets; keep files out of the repo | `REFERENCES` list in the batch output |
| Locations | Permission for private property; no recognizable third-party brands without clearance | Shot notes |
| Children | Avoid unless policy, law and guardians allow; never in restricted categories | Release with guardian signature |

## 7. Policy traps specific to video

| Trap | Safer approach |
|------|----------------|
| Personal attributes in the hook text or VO ("Are you overweight?") on Meta | Speak to the situation or the product |
| Before and after in weight loss, health, many cosmetics | Avoid, or follow the platform policy exactly; never generated |
| Fake UI: play buttons, notifications, system dialogs, fake close buttons | Native-looking text is fine; deceptive UI is not |
| Shocking or sensational openers | Pattern interrupts that are not shocking or misleading |
| Unlicensed trending audio in ads | Licensed tracks or platform commercial libraries only |
| Countdown or scarcity that is not real | Real deadlines and stock only |
| Health, finance, alcohol, gambling, dating, supplements | Market by market rules; human sign-off; special ad categories set by channel agents |
| Political or social issue content | Route to the human |

## 8. Disclosure wording templates

| Situation | On screen (first appearance, inside safe zone) | Caption or primary text |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------|
| AI presenter (synthetic performer) | "AI-generated presenter" (US, EU); California from 2027: "This performance features a synthetic digital performer." | "This video uses an AI-generated presenter." |
| Digital twin of a real person | "AI-generated video of <name>" | "Script approved by <name>." |
| AI scene or setting | "Scene created with AI" | "Visuals created with AI for illustration." |
| AI product depiction (only if allowed at all) | "AI illustration. Actual product may differ." prefer real product instead | Same |
| Actors portraying customers | "Actor portrayal" or "Dramatization" | Same |
| Paid creator | "Paid partnership with <Brand>" or "#ad" in the first seconds | "#ad" first; platform partnership label on |
| Sped up | "Sped up 2x" | |
| Results vary | "Results vary. Typical result: <x>" (substantiated) | Same |
| Mechanism illustration | "Illustration" | |

Disclosures must be legible (at least caption size, high contrast), in the ad's language, inside the safe zone and on screen long enough to read (the whole appearance of a synthetic performer).

## 9. Compliance packet (sent to `compliance` at P7)

1. List of files with ad names and flags.
2. Every claim with its exact words, timecode, file and `brand/CLAIMS.md` ID (approved only; others blocked).
3. Every number on screen with source and date.
4. Every person on screen: real (release ID), actor (contract), synthetic (tool, disclosure text and timing).
5. Every generated or AI-edited shot with tool, purpose and label plan per platform and market.
6. Music and asset licenses.
7. Offer terms and end date.
8. Markets where the ad will run (drives EU, New York, Hawaii, California, other local rules).

Any "no" in the packet blocks delivery. Record the compliance result and date in the batch output.
