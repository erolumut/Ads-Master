# Promo Calendar and Sales Events

> A promo calendar is a budget of discount days. Plan it a year ahead, protect full price periods, and respect the reference price rules that every promotion creates for the next one.

## 1. Calendar design rules
1. Set a promo budget per year: number of sitewide promo days, maximum depth, and a target discount rate (discounts / gross sales at list). Record it in `STRATEGY.md` via `growth-orchestrator`.
2. Choose 3 to 5 tent-pole moments per market where demand is highest and competitors discount; skip the rest or run value-add offers (gift, bundle, shipping upgrade) instead of price cuts.
3. Keep at least 30 days of stable full price before any EU or UK price reduction claim (the prior price is the lowest of the previous 30 days). In Turkey the advertised discount reference is the lowest price in the prior 10 days since 2026-08-01 (price tag rules since 2025-10-11) [Official, 2026-07, via secondary sources]. Plan pre-event prices accordingly; never raise prices before an event to inflate the discount.
4. Alternate offer types (price, gift, bundle, early access, free shipping) so customers do not learn to wait for one predictable discount.
5. Lock creative, feed, site and inventory dates backwards from the event (section 5).
6. Run a post-mortem within 14 days of every tent-pole (template in [Testing and measurement](offer-testing-and-measurement.md)).

## 8. Calendar health checks (monthly)
- [ ] Discount rate vs target, by month and channel.
- [ ] Share of revenue sold on promotion (promo dependency); rising share with flat total revenue is a warning.
- [ ] Days at full price before each planned reduction meet the market rule.
- [ ] No overlapping promos that stack unintentionally.
- [ ] Retail partner and marketplace promo windows aligned (see [Channel conflict](channel-conflict-and-price-parity.md)).
- [ ] Post-mortems completed for last month's events; learnings in `memory/offer-strategy.md` only when confirmed.
