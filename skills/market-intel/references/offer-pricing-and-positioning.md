# Offer, Pricing and Positioning

How to tear down competitor offers, monitor prices and promotions, map positioning, and run win and loss analysis. Outputs feed growth-orchestrator (economics), cro (pages), creative-strategy (angles) and commerce-feeds (Shopping competitiveness).

## 1. Offer teardown
An offer is everything the customer gets and risks, not just the price.

| Component | What to capture | Example |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|
| Core product or plan | What is included | 3 month supply; Pro plan with 5 seats |
| Price | List price, effective price after discounts, currency, tax display | EUR 49, EUR 39.20 with first order code |
| Discount mechanics | Depth, conditions, frequency | 20% first order; sitewide 30% on last weekend of month |
| Bundle and quantity breaks | Tiers and savings | Buy 2 save 15% |
| Shipping | Threshold, speed, cost | Free over EUR 40, 2 to 3 days |
| Financing | Installments, BNPL | 12 installments (Turkey), Klarna, Tabby |
| Trial or sample | Length, card required | 14 day trial, no card |
| Guarantee | Type and length | 60 day money back |
| Bonuses | Gifts, onboarding, content | Free onboarding call |
| Subscription terms | Cadence, cancellation | Monthly, cancel anytime |
| Urgency or scarcity | Real or manufactured | Countdown, limited stock |
| Proof | Ratings, review counts, logos, awards, certifications | 4.7 from 18k reviews |
| Risk signals | Hidden fees, auto renewals, complaints | Complaints about cancellation |

Procedure:
1. Capture the offer from the landing page each competitor uses in ads (from ad libraries), not only the homepage.
2. Capture the checkout up to the payment step to see fees and shipping (do not complete purchases unless the human approves a mystery shop budget; never use false identities).
3. Record date, market, URL and screenshots.
4. Build the offer matrix (section 6 template) and compute effective price per unit.

## 2. Price monitoring
| Method | Use for | Cadence |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Merchant Center price competitiveness and benchmark data | Our products vs market benchmark prices in Shopping | Weekly (ecommerce) |
| Manual capture of hero SKUs or plans | 10 to 30 key items | Monthly, weekly in peaks |
| Price tracking software (for example Prisync, Price2Spy, Competera) | Large catalogs | Daily |
| Marketplace price history (for example Keepa for Amazon) | Amazon competitors | As needed |
| Page change monitors (for example Visualping, Distill) | SaaS pricing pages, promo pages | Daily alerts |
| Wayback Machine | Historical pricing pages and offers | Quarterly or ad hoc |
| Promotional email archives and a dedicated inbox subscribed to competitor newsletters | Promo calendar and depth | Continuous |

Price index per SKU = our effective price / median competitor effective price x 100. Report distribution: share of SKUs above 110, 90 to 110, below 90. Margin impact for any proposed change goes to growth-orchestrator.

Promotion calendar: log competitor promotions (start, end, depth, channel, mechanic) for 12 months to predict the next season. Seasonal workflows (BFCM, Ramadan, 11.11) use it.

## 3. Positioning analysis
### 3.1 Positioning map
1. Choose two axes that buyers actually use to decide (from VoC decision criteria), for example price level vs specialization, ease vs power, speed vs customization.
2. Place each competitor using evidence: price data, feature lists, review themes, ad angles.
3. Mark white space and crowded zones.
4. Validate with 5 to 10 customer conversations or a survey before acting.

### 3.2 Competitive positioning statement check (based on April Dunford's method, "Obviously Awesome", 2019)
| Element | Question | Our answer | Competitor answers |
|---------|----------|-----------|--------------------|
| Competitive alternatives | What would customers do if we did not exist? | | |
| Unique attributes | What do we have that alternatives lack? | | |
| Value | What does that enable for customers (with proof)? | | |
| Best fit customers | Who cares most about that value? | | |
| Market category | What frame makes the value obvious? | | |

### 3.4 Claims comparison
| Claim type | Us (approved?) | Competitors | Risk |
|------------|----------------|-------------|------|
| Superiority ("best", "number one") | | | Needs substantiation; often restricted |
| Comparative ("faster than X") | | | Comparative advertising law and platform policy |
| Health, finance, environmental claims | | | Regulated in most markets |
Comparative advertising is generally permitted in the EU when objective, verifiable and not misleading (Directive 2006/114/EC), allowed in the US when truthful and substantiated, and permitted in Turkey under conditions in the commercial advertising regulation [Practitioner consensus; legal review required]. Platforms also restrict trademark use in ad text. Route any comparative claim to the human and BRAND.md.

## 4. Win and loss analysis
### 4.1 B2B and lead gen
1. Sample: closed won and closed lost deals from the last 30 to 90 days; 10 to 20 interviews per quarter, balanced won and lost [Practitioner consensus].
2. Interviewer: someone outside the sales team (or a third party) to get honest answers.
3. Guide (30 minutes):
   - What triggered the search? What were you using before?
   - Who was involved? What alternatives did you consider, and how did you find them (search, AI assistant, peers, reviews)?
   - What made you shortlist us? What nearly stopped you?
   - How did pricing compare? What did the winner do better?
   - Which content, ads or reviews influenced you?
4. Code reasons: product fit, price or value, trust or proof, sales experience, timing, integration, competitor strength, internal politics.
5. Compare with CRM loss reasons (often defaulted to "price"); report the gap.
6. Output: top 5 win reasons, top 5 loss reasons with frequencies and verbatims, competitor specific battlecard updates, recommendations (messaging, offer, product, sales process).

### 4.2 Ecommerce and app equivalents
- Post purchase survey: "What almost stopped you from buying?", "Which other brands did you consider?", "Where did you first hear about us?"
- Exit or cart abandonment survey: "What is stopping you today?"
- Churn survey for subscriptions: reason codes plus verbatim.
- Review mining of competitor 1 to 3 star reviews for loss reasons in the category (voice-of-customer-mining.md).

## 7. Turning findings into actions
| Finding | Action | Owner |
|---------|--------|-------|
| We are 15% above median price with similar offer | Test value framing, bundle, guarantee; or price test | growth-orchestrator, cro |
| Competitors all discount; we do not | Test non price value (shipping speed, guarantee) before discounting | cro, creative-strategy |
| Competitor guarantee stronger | Test matching guarantee with margin model | growth-orchestrator |
| White space on positioning map validated by VoC | New angle and landing page | creative-strategy, cro |
| Loss reason "integration missing" dominates | Product feedback; comparison content | human, seo |

## 8. Pitfalls
- Comparing list prices instead of effective prices after discounts, shipping and fees.
- Positioning maps on axes buyers do not use.
- Win and loss run by the sales rep who lost the deal.
- Copying a competitor's discount calendar without checking margin.
- Making comparative claims without substantiation and legal review.
