# Supplements, Cosmetics, Medical and Weight Loss

> Knowledge as of 2026-10. Rule pack for food supplements, cosmetics, medicines, medical devices, health services, telehealth and weight loss. Nutrition and health claim mechanics for foods (which also cover supplements in the EU, UK and Turkey) are in [Food and nutrition claims](food-and-nutrition-claims.md). Platform specifics are in [Platform ad policies](platform-ad-policies.md). Every new claim in this pack is NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW unless it is already an Approved row in CLAIMS.md.

## 1. Classification first

The same product can be a food supplement, a cosmetic, a medical device or a medicine depending on the claims made. Claims decide the regime, so a wrong claim can turn a supplement into an unauthorised medicine.

| If the copy says or implies | Likely regime | Consequence |
|-----------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Prevents, treats, cures, diagnoses a disease | Medicine (EU Directive 2001/83/EC Art 1(2); US FD&C Act drug definition; TR medicinal product law) | Unauthorised medicine; ad BLOCKED |
| Restores, corrects or modifies physiological functions by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic action | Medicine (EU functional definition) | NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW |
| Supports a normal body function with an authorised wording | Food supplement (EU Directive 2002/46/EC plus Reg 1924/2006) | Allowed with conditions |
| Cleans, perfumes, changes appearance, protects, keeps in good condition (external parts, teeth, mucous membranes) | Cosmetic (EU Reg 1223/2009 Art 2) | Claims under Reg 655/2013 |
| Diagnoses, monitors, treats, alleviates by physical means (apps included) | Medical device (EU MDR 2017/745) | Claims must match the intended purpose and CE marking |
| "Wrinkle elimination", "hair regrowth", "SPF", "anti-dandruff" (US) | US: OTC drug or drug claims, depending on wording | US drug rules; NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW |

## 2. Food supplements

| Rule ID | Rule | Source |
|---------|------|--------|
| SUPP-EU-01 | Supplements are foods: only authorised nutrition and health claims (see food pack). Labelling and advertising must not attribute disease prevention, treatment or cure properties, and must not state or imply that a varied diet cannot supply adequate nutrients | Directive 2002/46/EC Art 6; Reg 1924/2006 [Official, canonical] |
| SUPP-EU-02 | Botanical health claims "on hold" are not authorised claims; use is [Contested] after CJEU C-386/23 (2025-04-30). Default: NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW | EFSA; CJEU C-386/23 |
| SUPP-EU-03 | Doctor or health professional recommendation for foods and supplements is a prohibited health claim (Art 12(c)), except recognised national associations (Art 11) | Reg 1924/2006 |
| SUPP-US-01 | Structure/function claims (DSHEA, FD&C 403(r)(6)): must be truthful, not mislead, carry the disclaimer "This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease." and be notified to FDA within 30 days of first marketing | 21 CFR 101.93 [Official, canonical] |
| SUPP-US-02 | FTC requires competent and reliable scientific evidence, generally randomized controlled human trials on the product or an essentially equivalent formula, for health benefit claims in ads | FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance 2022-12 [Official] |
| SUPP-US-03 | FTC TruHeight order (final 2026-07-14/15): height growth supplements for children and teens, unsubstantiated claims plus fake and incentivized reviews; USD 750,000 paid (USD 4 million judgment partly suspended) | FTC press release 2026-07 [Official] |
| SUPP-UK-01 | GB retained claims regime; CAP Code section 15 (food, food supplements) and 12 (medicines and health); no claims that a supplement can treat or prevent disease | CAP Code [Official] |
| SUPP-TR-01 | Health claims only from the TİTCK guide annexes with their full text (since 2023); ads must not claim disease treatment, prevention or cure; no doctor, dentist, veterinarian or pharmacist image, statement or implication; pharmacy logos treated as health professional endorsement; no satisfaction or thank you letters or personal experience stories; "mucizevi" (miraculous) and "garantili" style wording repeatedly sanctioned; supplement may not be presented as a medicine; burden of proof on the advertiser | TİTCK guide 2023; Reklam Kurulu decisions; CBC Law and Patika Hukuk summaries [Official via secondary] |
| SUPP-TR-02 | From 2026-08-01 (amendment published RG 33297, 2026-07-01): ads must not give the impression that supplements replace normal foods; the absolute ban on comparative supplement ads was lifted (comparisons allowed except on health claim matters); consumer reviews containing non compliant health claims may not be published | Ticari Reklam ve Haksız Ticari Uygulamalar Yönetmeliği as amended [Official via law firm summaries] |
| SUPP-TR-03 | Enforcement examples: 2020 fines of TRY 104,781 each on 6 celebrities and the advertiser for an "immune strengthening" supplement; Nestlé Türkiye fined TRY 550,059 for using "Vitamin D supports normal immunity" where the condition was not met; February 2024 Reklam Kurulu meeting: 157 of 185 files found unlawful, TRY 24.0 million fines, 47 files on health related ads | AA 2020; Ticaret Bakanlığı 2024 [Official] |

## 3. Cosmetics

| Rule ID | Rule | Source |
|---------|------|--------|
| COSM-EU-01 | Text, names, trade marks, pictures and signs must not imply characteristics or functions the product does not have | Reg (EU) 1223/2009 Art 20 [Official] |
| COSM-EU-02 | Six common criteria for every claim: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed decision-making. Claims that the product has received authorisation from a competent authority are not allowed; claims that a product has a benefit when it only meets minimum legal requirements are not allowed | Reg (EU) 655/2013 Annex [Official] |
| COSM-EU-03 | Technical document on cosmetic claims (Commission, non binding but applied by authorities; Annexes III and IV apply since 2019-07-01): free-from claims not allowed for ingredients banned by law (for example "free from corticosteroids"); not allowed when they denigrate legal ingredients (the document gives "free from parabens" as not acceptable); "free from preservatives" only if no ingredient has a preserving function; "hypoallergenic" only where the product is designed to minimise allergenic potential and data confirm a very low allergenic potential; no implication of zero allergy risk | Commission technical document [Official, canonical] |
| COSM-EU-04 | No claims change from the Omnibus VI simplification (provisional agreement 2026-06-17: ingredient approvals, digital glossary, CMR derogations). Fragrance allergen labelling expanded by Reg (EU) 2023/1545: new products from 2026-07-31, existing stock until 2028-07-31; affects "fragrance-free" and "hypoallergenic" evidence | BeautyMatter, CIRS 2026 [Official via secondary] |
| COSM-EU-05 | Before and after and filtered images: must reflect the product's real effect; ASA has upheld complaints where social media filters exaggerated results (2021 onward). Production techniques that alter the result shown (retouching skin for a foundation ad) are misleading if they exaggerate the effect | ASA rulings [Official]; CAP guidance |
| COSM-US-01 | Intended use decides cosmetic vs drug: "treats acne", "regrows hair", "repairs damaged skin cells", "SPF" are drug claims; MoCRA (2022) adds facility registration, product listing and adverse event reporting, not claim approval | FDA [Official, canonical] |
| COSM-TR-01 | Kozmetik Yönetmeliği mirrors the EU regime; TİTCK supervises; claims follow EU common criteria in practice | [Practitioner consensus] |

Common cosmetic claim verdicts:

| Claim | Default verdict | Evidence needed |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| "Dermatologically tested" | APPROVED WITH EDITS if a test exists | Test report; do not imply "approved by dermatologists" |
| "Clinically proven to reduce wrinkles by 30%" | NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW | Study on this formula, population, method, statistics; US drug claim risk with "reduce wrinkles" permanently |
| "Paraben-free" | BLOCKED in EU (denigrating) unless counsel approves | n/a |
| "Chemical-free", "non-toxic" | BLOCKED (everything is chemical; implies others are toxic) | n/a |
| "Hypoallergenic" | NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW | Allergenic potential data and post market data |
| "Vegan", "cruelty-free" | APPROVED WITH EDITS with certificate | Certificate; EU animal testing ban means "not tested on animals" alone presents a legal requirement as a feature (risk under UCPD) |
| "Natural" | NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW | Percentage of natural origin per ISO 16128, stated |

## 4. Medicines, medical devices and health services

| Rule ID | Rule | Source |
|---------|------|--------|
| MED-EU-01 | No advertising of prescription medicines to the general public. OTC medicine ads must be clearly identified as ads, encourage rational use, include required information, and must not suggest that effects are guaranteed, free of side effects, or better than another treatment, or use recommendations by scientists, health professionals or celebrities | Directive 2001/83/EC Art 88 to 90 [Official, canonical] |
| MED-EU-02 | Medical devices: claims must not mislead about intended purpose, safety or performance, or imply properties the device does not have | Reg (EU) 2017/745 Art 7 [Official] |
| MED-DE-01 | Germany Heilmittelwerbegesetz (HWG): restrictions on consumer advertising for medicines, devices and treatments, including before and after images for cosmetic surgery without medical necessity | HWG [Official, canonical; verify paragraph per case] |
| MED-UK-01 | Prescription-only medicines (POMs) may not be advertised to the public (CAP 12.12); celebrity or clinician endorsement of medicines breaches CAP 12.18. Weight loss POMs: Enforcement Notice with MHRA and GPhC (2025-09); terms such as "weight loss injections", "weight loss pen", "GLP-1" and imagery of unbranded pens or vials are treated as POM references; compliance reached 99% by 2026-01 after targeted action; first influencer posts banned 2026-02; first oral weight loss medicine ad bans early 2026-10 (MedExpress, Juniper, The Care Pharmacy) | ASA 2026; ITV 2026-10-06 [Official] |
| MED-US-01 | Prescription drug ads: FDA rules (21 CFR 202.1). HHS and FDA DTC crackdown announced 2025-09-09 (thousands of letters, over 100 enforcement letters, telehealth and social media in scope). A proposed rule to remove the broadcast "adequate provision" option (RIN 0910-AJ14) is on the agenda for December 2026; not proposed yet as of 2026-10 | HHS fact sheet 2025-09; Foley Hoag 2026-07 [Official] |
| MED-US-02 | Compounded GLP-1 marketing by telehealth: FDA warning letters in waves (2025-09; about 30 letters dated 2026-02-20, announced 2026-03-03; 25 letters 2026-06-16). Violations cited: implying sameness with FDA-approved drugs, "FDA-approved" sourcing language, branding compounded drugs as the telehealth company's product. FDA launched a compliance page for telehealth firms | NatLawReview 2026-03; telehealth.org 2026-06 [Official via secondary] |
| MED-TR-01 | Prescription medicine advertising to the public is banned; health services (clinics, doctors) operate under strict promotion limits from the Ministry of Health (a 2025 promotion and information regulation is reported); no patient testimonials, no comparative or attracting promotion | [Practitioner consensus]; 2025 regulation [Unverified] |

## 5. Weight loss and body image

| Rule ID | Rule | Source |
|---------|------|--------|
| WL-EU-01 | Foods and supplements: no reference to rate or amount of weight loss (Art 12(b)). Authorised weight claims exist only for specific substances and meal replacements with strict conditions (for example glucomannan) | Reg 1924/2006; Reg 432/2012 |
| WL-UK-01 | CAP Code section 13 (weight control and slimming): not directed at under 18s; no claims that a precise amount of weight can be lost in a stated period or that weight can be lost from specific body parts; obesity treatment only under suitable medical supervision; Meta, TikTok and Google also restrict targeting to 18+ | CAP Code 13 [Official, canonical; verify rule numbers] |
| WL-US-01 | FTC "Gut Check" red flag claims (never substantiable): weight loss of 2 lbs or more a week for a month or more without diet or exercise; substantial weight loss no matter what or how much the consumer eats; permanent weight loss; blocking fat or calorie absorption for substantial weight loss; safely losing more than 3 lbs a week for more than four weeks; substantial weight loss for all users; substantial weight loss by wearing a product or rubbing it on the skin | FTC Gut Check guidance (2014) [Official, canonical] |
| WL-TR-01 | Weight loss promises in supplement ads are a standard Reklam Kurulu finding; avoid amounts, timeframes and "zayıflatır" type wording without an authorised claim | Reklam Kurulu decisions [Official via secondary] |
| WL-PLAT-01 | Meta Health and Wellness standard (policy date 2026-07-22): weight loss, cosmetic procedure and supplement ads 18+; copy must not disparage appearance; no pinched fat close-ups, no sensational timeframes without qualifiers. Before and after for cosmetic products and procedures allowed for 18+ audiences; whether weight loss before and after is now allowed is [Contested] across sources: treat as NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW. TikTok updated its weight management and body image policy (2026-05) and weight loss and muscle gain policy (2026-07). OpenAI disallows wellness products with unsupported claims (diet pills, detox). LinkedIn prohibits misleading diet and weight loss claims | Meta Transparency Center; TikTok changelog 2026; OpenAI Ad Policies v1.6 [Official] |

## 6. Telehealth and online pharmacy advertising

1. Certification first: Google requires LegitScript (or national) certification for online pharmacies and telehealth that prescribe; Spain allows certified telemedicine providers to promote prescription drug services from 2026-08 without naming drugs; India allowed certified online pharmacies to promote prescription drug services (2026-04) [Official, Google Ads policy updates]. Meta requires LegitScript certification for online pharmacies and prescription drug advertising.
2. OpenAI ChatGPT ads: health services restricted to approved US advertisers; prescription and OTC medicines, telehealth and online pharmacies not allowed [Official, OpenAI Ad Policies v1.6, 2026-09-10].
3. Microsoft Advertising restructured its healthcare policy on 2026-07-01 (market specific section covering 130+ markets; online pharmacy pre-approval required since 2026-01); consumer targeted ads for sensitive health conditions are not allowed [Official via PPC News Feed].
4. Never name a POM in UK consumer ads, social posts or emails; never show injection pens.
5. Health data from these funnels is sensitive: see [Privacy in marketing](privacy-in-marketing-and-consent.md).

## 7. Review checklist for this pack

| # | Check | Fail action |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Product classification documented (food, supplement, cosmetic, device, medicine, service) | NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW |
| 2 | No disease, diagnosis or cure language anywhere (copy, images, product name, hashtags, alt text) | BLOCKED |
| 3 | Health claims match authorised wording and conditions for each market | BLOCKED or rewrite |
| 4 | US supplement structure/function claims carry the DSHEA disclaimer; notification on file | APPROVED WITH EDITS |
| 5 | No health professional endorsement for foods and supplements (EU, TR) | BLOCKED |
| 6 | No weight loss amount or timeframe | BLOCKED |
| 7 | Before and after visuals typical, unretouched, platform allowed, market allowed | NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW |
| 8 | Testimonials genuine, typical or with typical results disclosed (US), not used for supplements in TR | BLOCKED if fake |
| 9 | Audience 18+ for weight loss, supplements, cosmetic procedures on Meta, TikTok, Google | Fix targeting via channel agent |
| 10 | Cosmetic free-from and hypoallergenic claims meet the technical document | Rewrite |
| 11 | Medicines: no POM advertising to the public; OTC ads carry required information | BLOCKED |
| 12 | Telehealth: certification valid in each market; no "FDA approved" for compounded drugs | BLOCKED |

## 8. Worked examples

| Copy | Market | Verdict | Rule | Fix |
|------|--------|---------|------|-----|
| "Our magnesium gummies reduce anxiety" | EU | BLOCKED | SUPP-EU-01 | "Magnesium contributes to normal psychological function" if at least a source of magnesium |
| "Supports normal immune function*" with DSHEA disclaimer | US | APPROVED WITH EDITS | SUPP-US-01 | Confirm FDA notification and substantiation file |
| Pharmacist in white coat holding the supplement | TR | BLOCKED | SUPP-TR-01 | Product only creative |
| "Hypoallergenic face cream" | EU | NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW | COSM-EU-03 | Provide allergenic potential evidence |
| "Get your weight loss jab today" | UK | BLOCKED | MED-UK-01 | Promote the consultation service without referring to POMs or pens |
| "Lose 10 lbs in 10 days" | US | BLOCKED | WL-US-01 | Remove |
| Before and after photo, cosmetic laser treatment, 18+ targeting | Meta, UK | NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW | WL-PLAT-01, COSM-EU-05 | Typicality and consent evidence; check national medical advertising rules |

## 9. Templates

```text
US supplement structure/function disclaimer (21 CFR 101.93), with an asterisk on each claim:
*These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

EU health claim accompanying statement (Reg 1924/2006 Art 10(2)), adapt per product:
Food supplements should not be used as a substitute for a varied and balanced diet and a healthy lifestyle. Take 1 capsule daily. Do not exceed the recommended daily dose.

EU OTC medicine ad minimum (Directive 2001/83/EC Art 89), adapt per product and country:
<Name> contains <active substance>. Always read the label/leaflet.
```
