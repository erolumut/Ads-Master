# Food and Nutrition Claims

> Knowledge as of 2026-10. Rule pack for food, drink and food supplement nutrition claims in the EU, UK, US and Turkey. Legal texts cited as "canonical" were not re-fetched in the 2026-10 sweep: check the consolidated text before relying on an exact figure for a new claim. Supplements, health products and weight loss continue in [Supplements, cosmetics and health](supplements-cosmetics-and-health.md).

## 1. How to use this pack

1. Identify every nutrition claim (statement that a food has a beneficial nutritional property) and every health claim (statement that a relationship exists between a food and health) in the asset, including images, product names and brand names.
2. For each claim find the rule ID below and the condition of use.
3. Check the condition against the nutrition table in PRODUCT_FACTS (per 100 g or 100 ml as sold, unless the rule says otherwise).
4. Copy the exact authorised wording or a wording with the same meaning to the consumer (EU Art 13 lists allow "wording with the same meaning"; national authorities publish flexibility guidance). When in doubt keep the register wording.
5. Add mandatory accompanying statements.

## 2. EU core rules (Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006)

| Rule ID | Rule | Practical effect | Source |
|---------|------|------------------|--------|
| FOOD-EU-01 | General principles: claims must not be false, ambiguous or misleading; must not raise doubt about other foods; must not encourage excess consumption; must not state or imply that a balanced diet cannot provide adequate nutrients; must not exploit fear through changes in bodily functions | Applies to every claim, image and brand name | Art 3 [Official, canonical] |
| FOOD-EU-02 | Only nutrition claims listed in the Annex, under its conditions | Threshold table in section 3 | Art 8 and Annex [Official, canonical] |
| FOOD-EU-03 | Health claims are prohibited unless authorised and on the EU Register (Art 13(1) function claims in Reg (EU) 432/2012 and later acts; Art 14 disease risk reduction and children's development claims) | Use only register wording with its conditions of use | Art 10(1), 13, 14; EU Register |
| FOOD-EU-04 | A health claim must be accompanied (on the label, or in presentation and advertising where labelling is absent) by: the importance of a varied and balanced diet and a healthy lifestyle; the quantity and pattern of consumption needed for the effect; where appropriate a statement for persons who should avoid the food; a warning for products likely to present a health risk if consumed to excess | Ads that make a health claim carry the statement or link to it | Art 10(2) |
| FOOD-EU-05 | General, non specific health benefit references ("good for you", "supports wellbeing", "boosts immunity") are only allowed when accompanied by a specific authorised health claim | Pair "immune support" with, for example, "Vitamin C contributes to the normal function of the immune system" if the product qualifies | Art 10(3); Commission Implementing Decision 2013/63/EU |
| FOOD-EU-06 | Prohibited health claims: suggesting health could be affected by not consuming the food; referring to the rate or amount of weight loss; referring to recommendations of individual doctors or health professionals (other than recognised national associations, Art 11) | No "lose 3 kg in a month"; no "recommended by Dr X" | Art 12 |
| FOOD-EU-07 | Disease claims: food information must not attribute to a food the property of preventing, treating or curing a human disease | BLOCK "prevents colds", "treats diabetes" | Reg (EU) 1169/2011 Art 7(3) |
| FOOD-EU-08 | Beverages above 1.2% alcohol: no health claims; nutrition claims only for low alcohol, reduced alcohol or reduced energy | Beer ads cannot say "rich in B vitamins" | Art 4(3) |
| FOOD-EU-09 | Botanical "on hold" claims (roughly 1,500 to 2,000 claims; counts differ by source) were never assessed. | FOOD-EU-10 | "Probiotic": the Commission treats the term as a health claim; no probiotic claim has been authorised; some member states (reported: France, Italy, Spain, Czechia, Bulgaria) tolerate the term under conditions | EU default: strain name and count only | European Ombudsman decision 2273/2023/MIK (2024-12-20) [Official]; national tolerance [Unverified] |

## 3. EU nutrition claim thresholds (Annex to Reg 1924/2006, as amended by Reg 116/2010 and Reg 1047/2012)

Conditions are per 100 g or 100 ml unless stated. [Official, canonical text]

| Claim | Condition |
|-------|-----------|
| Low energy | Max 40 kcal (170 kJ)/100 g solids or 20 kcal (80 kJ)/100 ml liquids; table-top sweeteners 4 kcal per portion (sweetness of 6 g sucrose) |
| Energy-reduced | Energy reduced by at least 30%, stating the characteristic(s) causing the reduction |
| Energy-free | Max 4 kcal (17 kJ)/100 ml; table-top sweeteners 0.4 kcal per portion |
| Low fat | Max 3 g fat/100 g solids or 1.5 g/100 ml liquids (1.8 g/100 ml for semi-skimmed milk) |
| Fat-free | Max 0.5 g fat/100 g or 100 ml. Claims expressed as "X% fat-free" are prohibited |
| Low saturated fat | Saturated plus trans fat max 1.5 g/100 g solids or 0.75 g/100 ml liquids, and max 10% of energy |
| Saturated fat-free | Saturated plus trans fat max 0.1 g/100 g or 100 ml |
| Low sugars | Max 5 g sugars/100 g solids or 2.5 g/100 ml liquids |
| Sugars-free | Max 0.5 g sugars/100 g or 100 ml |
| With no added sugars | No added mono- or disaccharides or any other food used for its sweetening properties. If sugars occur naturally, the label also states "Contains naturally occurring sugars" |
| Low sodium / low salt | Max 0.12 g sodium (or equivalent salt) per 100 g or 100 ml; waters other than natural mineral waters max 2 mg sodium/100 ml |
| Very low sodium / salt | Max 0.04 g sodium (or salt equivalent) per 100 g or 100 ml; not for natural mineral waters and other waters |
| Sodium-free / salt-free | Max 0.005 g sodium (or salt equivalent) per 100 g |
| No added sodium / salt | No added sodium or salt or ingredient containing added sodium or salt, and max 0.12 g sodium (or salt equivalent) per 100 g or 100 ml |
| Source of fibre | At least 3 g fibre/100 g or at least 1.5 g fibre/100 kcal |
| High fibre | At least 6 g fibre/100 g or at least 3 g fibre/100 kcal |
| Source of protein | At least 12% of the energy value of the food is provided by protein |
| High protein | At least 20% of the energy value of the food is provided by protein |
| Source of [vitamin/mineral] | At least a significant amount: 15% of the nutrient reference value (NRV) per 100 g or 100 ml for foods other than beverages, 7.5% per 100 ml for beverages, or 15% per portion for single portion packs (FIC Annex XIII Part A) |
| High [vitamin/mineral] | At least twice the "source of" value |
| Contains [nutrient or substance] | Meets all applicable provisions; for vitamins and minerals the "source of" condition applies |
| Increased [nutrient] | Meets "source of" and the increase is at least 30% compared with a similar product |
| Reduced [nutrient] | At least 30% less than a similar product; micronutrients a 10% difference in NRV; sodium or salt 25%. "Reduced saturated fat" also needs saturated plus trans at least 30% lower and trans not higher; "reduced sugars" also needs energy equal or lower than the similar product |
| Light / lite | Same conditions as "reduced", stating the characteristic(s) that make it light |
| Naturally / natural | Prefix allowed when the food naturally meets the condition of a claim |
| Source of omega-3 fatty acids | At least 0.3 g ALA per 100 g and per 100 kcal, or at least 40 mg EPA plus DHA per 100 g and per 100 kcal |
| High omega-3 fatty acids | At least 0.6 g ALA per 100 g and per 100 kcal, or at least 80 mg EPA plus DHA per 100 g and per 100 kcal |
| High monounsaturated fat | At least 45% of fatty acids from monounsaturated fat and monounsaturated fat gives more than 20% of energy |
| High polyunsaturated fat | At least 45% of fatty acids from polyunsaturated fat and polyunsaturated fat gives more than 20% of energy |
| High unsaturated fat | At least 70% of fatty acids from unsaturated fat and unsaturated fat gives more than 20% of energy |

### 3.1 Calculating protein energy
Energy conversion factors (FIC Annex XIV): protein 4 kcal/g, carbohydrate 4 kcal/g, fat 9 kcal/g, polyols 2.4 kcal/g, fibre 2 kcal/g, alcohol 7 kcal/g.

```
protein_energy_share = (protein_g_per_100g x 4) / energy_kcal_per_100g
Source of protein if share >= 0.12; High protein if share >= 0.20
```

| Product (per 100 g) | Protein | Energy | Share | Allowed |
|---------------------|---------|--------|-------|---------|
| Protein bar A | 14 g | 400 kcal | 14.0% | Source of protein |
| Protein bar B | 22 g | 380 kcal | 23.2% | High protein (source of protein also true) |
| Granola | 10 g | 450 kcal | 8.9% | No protein claim |
| Skyr | 11 g | 63 kcal | 69.8% | High protein |

### 3.2 Comparative nutrition claims
Comparisons ("30% less sugar") are only between foods of the same category, using a range of foods of that category, and must state the difference in quantity (Art 9). The comparator must be identified (for example "than our 2025 recipe" or "than the average of leading milk chocolates").

## 4. EU authorised health claims: how to use the register

1. Search the EU Register of nutrition and health claims (food.ec.europa.eu) for the nutrient or substance; note the exact wording, conditions of use and any restrictions.
2. Confirm the product meets the condition (most vitamin and mineral claims need at least "source of", that is 15% NRV per 100 g or per portion as specified).
3. Use the wording or a same meaning variant; keep the nutrient named ("Vitamin C contributes..." not "Our juice boosts immunity").
4. Add the Art 10(2) statements.

Examples [Official, Reg (EU) 432/2012 and register entries, canonical]:

| Claim | Condition of use (summary) |
|-------|---------------------------|
| Vitamin C contributes to the normal function of the immune system | Food is at least a source of vitamin C |
| Protein contributes to a growth in muscle mass | Food is at least a source of protein per the Annex |
| Calcium is needed for the maintenance of normal bones | At least a source of calcium |
| Beta-glucans from oats contribute to maintenance of normal blood cholesterol levels | At least 1 g oat beta-glucan per quantified portion; inform that the effect is with 3 g daily |
| Glucomannan, in the context of an energy restricted diet, contributes to weight loss | 1 g per quantified portion, 3 g daily in three 1 g doses with 1 to 2 glasses of water before meals |

EFSA opinions are not authorisations: a positive EFSA opinion (for example the 2026-02 oat beta-glucan postprandial glucose evaluation) can only be used after a Commission implementing act. [Official, EFSA; NutraIngredients 2026-01]

## 9. Decision tree for a food claim

```
1. Does it mention a disease (prevent, treat, cure)?  -> BLOCKED (FOOD-EU-07, FOOD-US-06, FOOD-TR-04)
2. Is it a nutrition claim?
   2a. Is the wording on the Annex list (or US 101.x, TR list)?  no -> BLOCKED or rewrite to listed wording
   2b. Does PRODUCT_FACTS meet the threshold?                    no -> BLOCKED; suggest the claim it does meet
   2c. Mandatory extra text (naturally occurring sugars, characteristic for light/reduced)? -> APPROVED WITH EDITS
3. Is it a health claim (function, benefit, wellbeing)?
   3a. General benefit only ("good for you")? -> needs a specific authorised claim next to it (FOOD-EU-05)
   3b. Authorised wording and condition met? -> add Art 10(2) statements -> APPROVED WITH EDITS
   3c. Botanical, probiotic, or not on the register? -> NEEDS HUMAN OR LEGAL REVIEW
4. Is it a status claim (organic, vegan, gluten-free, halal, origin)? -> verified certificate fact? yes APPROVED, no BLOCKED
5. UK paid online or TV before 21:00 and product LHF? -> brand-only creative or BLOCKED (FOOD-UK-02)
```
