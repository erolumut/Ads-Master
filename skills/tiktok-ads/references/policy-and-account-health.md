# Policy, Regulation and Account Health

> Knowledge as of 2026-10. TikTok's Advertising Policies vary by market and change often. Treat this module as a map of where risk lives, then read the current policy page for the exact market and category before launch. Category specifics marked [Unverified] were not re-checked in this research cycle.

## 1. Policy structure

| Layer | What it governs | Where |
|-------|----------------|-------|
| Advertising Policies: prohibited | Products and services that can never be advertised | TikTok Advertising Policies (by region) |
| Advertising Policies: restricted | Allowed with conditions (licenses, age 18+ or 21+, market limits, pre-approval) | Same, per industry |
| Ad creative and landing page rules | Claims, misleading content, sensational content, landing page function and match | Same |
| Community Guidelines | Apply to all content including Spark Ads posts | TikTok Community Guidelines |
| Branded Content Policy | Creator sponsored posts and disclosure | TikTok Branded Content Policy |
| TikTok Shop policies | Product listing, prohibited Shop products, seller conduct | Seller Center academy and policies |
| Regulation | DSA (EU), DMA, data protection, consumer protection, political ad laws | Laws per market |

## 3. Creative and landing page rules that cause most rejections [Practitioner consensus]
1. Exaggerated or unsubstantiated claims ("cure", "guaranteed results", "lose 10 kg in a week").
2. Before/after imagery for weight, skin or body outcomes.
3. Misleading UI (fake buttons, fake notifications, fake system alerts).
4. Price or offer mismatch between ad and landing page.
5. Landing page not functional, slow, geo-blocked to the reviewer, or missing contact, refund and privacy information.
6. Unlicensed music or copyrighted content.
7. Sensational, shocking or overly sexual content.
8. Targeting minors with restricted products.
9. Using a public figure's likeness without consent, including AI likenesses.
10. Undisclosed AI-generated realistic content or deceptive AI testimonials.

## 4. AI-generated content (AIGC) rules
- TikTok requires labeling realistic AI-generated content in posts [Official, Community Guidelines] and automatically labels some AI content using content credentials [Unverified current scope].
- Symphony Agent and Symphony Creative Studio outputs carry AI labels, invisible watermarks and content moderation filters [Official, 2026-06].
- Custom Avatars of real people require the person's identity verification and recorded consent [Unverified, third-party guide].
- Ads Master rules: disclose AI-generated realistic content, never fabricate testimonials or expert endorsements with AI, keep written consent for any likeness or voice, never imitate public figures.

## 5. Ad review and appeals
1. New and edited ads go through review, usually within hours and commonly within 24 hours [Practitioner consensus].
2. If rejected: read the rejection reason in Ads Manager. Fix and resubmit (preferred) or appeal if the rejection is wrong.
3. Appeal through the ad's rejection detail (appeal option) with a concise explanation and evidence (licenses, substantiation, landing page screenshots).
4. Track rejections per account. Repeated violations escalate to account restrictions.
5. Spark Ads can be rejected for content in the original post or for the creator's account status.

## 6. Account health

| Status or signal | Meaning | Action |
|------------------|---------|--------|
| Account under review | New account or billing verification | Wait; complete verification requests promptly |
| Ads rejected repeatedly | Policy risk building | Pause similar ads; fix root cause; review all live ads in that category |
| Account suspended | Policy, payment, suspicious activity, linked to banned assets | Recovery play (scaling-playbooks.md section 7.2) |
| Payment failure | Card declined or limit reached | Update payment; add backup method |
| Business verification requests | Identity or license checks for restricted categories | Provide documents fast; delays stop delivery |
| TikTok Shop violations | Seller health points, listing removals | Coordinate with Shop operator; Shop violations can stop GMV Max |

Prevention checklist:
- [ ] 2-step verification on all users; least-privilege roles
- [ ] Payment methods owned by the business; backup method on file
- [ ] Landing pages with contact details, refund policy, privacy policy, terms
- [ ] Category licenses and certificates on file for restricted categories
- [ ] Claim substantiation stored in BRAND.md or linked docs
- [ ] No reuse of assets (payment, domain, BC, identity) from suspended accounts
- [ ] Monthly review of rejected ads and policy updates

## 8. Brand safety controls
- Placement control: TikTok placements only, or manual placement selection in Smart+; TikTok Ad Network offers DoubleVerify and IAS brand safety and placement controls [Official, 2026-10].
- Inventory filters for In-Feed placements (full, standard, limited inventory tiers) where available [Unverified current names].
- Pulse uses vetted content adjacency.
- Comment controls on non-Spark ads; moderation of Spark Ad comments.
- Third-party verification (IAS, DoubleVerify, Zefr) for brand campaigns at Enterprise tier.
- Creator vetting (spark-ads-and-creators.md section 4).

## 9. Pre-launch policy checklist (run per campaign)
- [ ] Category status checked on the current TikTok Advertising Policies page for each target market (date checked recorded)
- [ ] Required licenses, certificates or pre-approvals submitted and approved
- [ ] Age and geo controls set and enforced where the category requires them
- [ ] Every claim in ad text, voiceover and on-screen text appears in BRAND.md approved claims with substantiation
- [ ] No before/after, body-shaming, guaranteed results, fake urgency timers or fake UI elements
- [ ] Music from CML or original audio; licenses on file for any voice, likeness or third-party footage
- [ ] AI-generated realistic content disclosed; no AI testimonials or AI public figures
- [ ] Landing page: works on mobile in the target market, matches offer and price, shows business identity, contact, refund and privacy policies
- [ ] Spark Ads posts carry branded content disclosure where paid
- [ ] TikTok Shop products comply with Shop prohibited and restricted product lists

## 10. Appeal templates

Ad rejection appeal (use only after fixing anything that was actually wrong):
```
Ad ID: <id> | Ad group: <name> | Rejection reason shown: <reason>
We believe this ad complies with [policy name] because:
1. <Specific fact, e.g. the claim "clinically tested" is supported by study X (attached summary)>
2. <Landing page shows business name, address, refund policy at URL>
3. <Targeting is limited to 18+ in <market> as required>
Attachments: <license / certificate / substantiation>
Request: please re-review the ad.
```

Account suspension appeal:
```
Advertiser ID: <id> | Business Center: <name> | Suspension date: <date> | Reason shown: <reason>
What we found: <one or two sentences, factual>
What we fixed: <removed ads X, Y; updated landing page; changed payment method owner to the business; enabled 2-step verification for all users>
Evidence: <screenshots, documents>
Commitment: <how we prevent recurrence: claims review step, policy checklist before launch>
Request: please review and restore the account.
```

Keep appeals short, factual and complete. Multiple partial appeals slow the review.

## 11. Landing page compliance checklist
| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Business identity | Company name, contact method visible |
| Policies | Privacy policy, terms, refund or return policy reachable from the page |
| Offer match | Price, discount and product in the ad match the page |
| Function | Loads on mobile, no broken checkout, no forced app download, no pop-up walls blocking content |
| Geo | Accessible from the targeted market (reviewers may check from that market) |
| Claims | Same substantiation standard as the ad |
| Restricted info | No collection of sensitive data without legal basis |
