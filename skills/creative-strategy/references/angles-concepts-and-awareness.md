# Angles, Concepts and Awareness

> Purpose: convert the VOC bank into a structured slate of angles and concepts that are genuinely different, matched to awareness levels, and ready to brief.

## 1. The hierarchy (use these words precisely)

| Level | Definition | Example (sleep supplement) | Changes how delivery treats it |
|-------|-----------|----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Persona | Who the ad speaks to | Shift working nurse | High: new persona usually means new audience pocket |
| Desire or pain | The core motivation | "I can't fall asleep after a night shift" | High |
| Angle | The argument or lens used to connect product to desire | "Your body clock is not broken, your light exposure is" (mechanism angle) | High |
| Concept | The creative idea that carries the angle | "Nurse films her 7am wind down routine" | High |
| Execution | A specific produced ad of a concept (format, talent, edit) | 30s UGC 9:16 with captions, talent A | Medium |
| Iteration | A variant of an execution (hook, first frame, CTA, length, music, text) | Same video, 5 different first 3 seconds | Low: likely grouped with the original |

A **new concept** changes at least two of: persona, desire or pain, angle, format, visual world (setting, look), talent, narrative structure. A new headline, color, music bed or CTA is an iteration. See [Andromeda and creative diversity](andromeda-and-creative-diversity.md).

## 2. Eugene Schwartz: awareness levels (Breakthrough Advertising, 1966)

| Level | The reader knows | Ad must | Hook pattern | Typical placement and audience |
|-------|------------------|--------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| Unaware | Not even that they have the problem | Lead with identity, story, curiosity, or a symptom they recognize | "If you wake up at 3am every night..." "Nurses, this one is for you" | Broad cold audiences, Reels, TikTok, YouTube |
| Problem aware | The problem, not the solutions | Agitate the problem, name it, then introduce a new way | "Rinsing twice and it still smells? Here's why." | Broad cold, search for problem queries |
| Solution aware | Solutions exist, not your product | Differentiate the mechanism, compare to alternatives | "Why melatonin stops working (and what does)" | Cold and warm, comparison queries |
| Product aware | Your product, not convinced | Proof, objections, risk reversal, comparison | "3,412 five star reviews and one complaint we fixed" | Warm, retargeting, branded search |
| Most aware | Wants it, needs a reason now | Offer, deadline, bundle, guarantee | "Ends Sunday: 2 for 1" | Retargeting, existing customers, email |

Rules:
- Broad delivery systems (Advantage+ sales, Smart+, PMax) put ads in front of every awareness level. Your concept mix must cover several levels, or the system will only find the narrow slice your ads speak to.
- Most accounts over-produce product aware and most aware ads (offer and feature ads). New customer growth usually comes from unaware and problem aware concepts. [Practitioner consensus]
- The less aware the audience, the longer the lead before the product appears, and the more the hook must be about them, not you.

## 3. Schwartz: market sophistication (stages 1 to 5)

| Stage | Market condition | Winning approach | Example claim style |
|-------|------------------|------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | First to market | Simple direct claim | "Lose weight" |
| 2 | Competitors copy the claim | Enlarge the claim | "Lose 10 lbs in 30 days" |
| 3 | Claims exhausted | Introduce a new mechanism | "The gut bacteria method" |
| 4 | Mechanisms copied | Enlarge or elaborate the mechanism | "The 3 strain gut method, 2x faster" |
| 5 | Market tired of claims | Identification: sell identity, story, experience | "For women who are done with diets" |

Diagnose the stage from the competitor angle map. Most mature DTC and SaaS categories are stage 4 or 5: mechanism plus identity concepts win, generic benefit claims lose. [Practitioner consensus]

## 4. Jobs to be done

**Job statement:** When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].

| Job layer | Question | Ad use |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| Functional | What task are they trying to get done? | Demo, how it works |
| Emotional | How do they want to feel? | Story, transformation, relief |
| Social | How do they want to be seen? | Identity, status, belonging |

**Forces of progress** (Moesta, Switch interviews)

| Force | Meaning | Creative use |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| Push | Pain of the current situation | Problem agitation hooks |
| Pull | Attraction of the new solution | Outcome, demo, transformation |
| Anxiety | Fear about the new solution | Objection handling, guarantees, proof, "what to expect" |
| Habit | Comfort with the current way | Switching cost removal, "takes 2 minutes", comparison with status quo |

A complete concept set addresses all four forces across different ads.

## 5. Desire and pain map

Build a table per persona:

| Persona | Pain (verbatim) | Desire (verbatim) | Trigger moment | Failed solutions | Objections | Proof we have | Awareness |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------|------------------|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| Shift nurse | "I lie there wired at 9am" | "Sleep like a normal person on my days off" | Starting night rotation | Melatonin, blackout curtains, wine | "Will I feel groggy?" | 412 reviews mention "no grogginess" | Problem aware |

Fill from the VOC bank. Empty proof cells mean either produce proof (survey, case study) or avoid the angle.

## 6. Angle generators (25 lenses)

Use these to produce angle candidates from each pain or desire row. Keep the ones backed by quotes.

1. Problem agitation: name the pain more vividly than they can.
2. Hidden cause or mechanism: "it's not X, it's Y".
3. Failed solutions: "you tried A, B, C; here's why they failed".
4. Identity: "for [specific person]".
5. Enemy: a villain (the industry, a habit, a myth, not a named competitor unless approved).
6. Us vs them: direct or category comparison.
7. Transformation: before and after state (watch platform policy on before/after imagery).
8. Day in the life: the product in a real routine.
9. Founder origin: why we built it.
10. Expert authority: credentialed person explains (real, consented, disclosed).
11. Social proof at scale: numbers, reviews, community.
12. Testimonial specific outcome: one customer, one measurable result.
13. Objection first: open with the top objection and answer it.
14. Myth busting: "everything you were told about X is wrong".
15. Cost of inaction: what staying put costs per month or year.
16. Price reframe: per day, versus alternatives, versus the cost of the problem.
17. Risk reversal: guarantee, free trial, returns.
18. Speed or ease: "in 2 minutes", "no setup".
19. Newness: new mechanism, new version, new ingredient (must be true).
20. Scarcity or timeliness: season, event, limited run (must be true).
21. Gift or other person benefit: buying for someone else.
22. Ritual or sensory: ASMR, texture, unboxing.
23. Education: teach something useful, product as the natural next step.
24. Comparison to an expensive alternative: "the $400 result for $40".
25. Community or movement: join people like you.

## 8. Concept scoring (pick what to brief)

Score each concept 1 to 5:

| Criterion | 1 | 5 |
|-----------|---|---|
| Insight strength | Generic benefit | Vivid verbatim pain with frequency evidence |
| Differentiation | Same as competitors' angle | White space in the angle map |
| Proof available | None | Strong reviews, data, demo, expert |
| Diversity contribution | Same persona, angle and format as live winners | Different persona and angle and format |
| Production cost (inverse) | Shoot, actors, location | Phone footage, existing assets, AI-assisted |

Priority = sum, with diversity contribution doubled when the Meta Creative diversity rating is Low or when live ads share one persona. Brief the top concepts that fit the tier's monthly concept count.

## 10. Worked example

Account: DTC odor removing laundry additive, Growth tier ($18k/month Meta, $4k TikTok), 4 live ads, all product aware feature statics, Meta Creative diversity rating Low.

1. VOC: 19% of 3 star competitor reviews mention "still smells after washing"; Reddit r/running threads "my gym clothes smell even after washing"; post-purchase survey top objection "Is it safe for my machine?"
2. Map: personas = runners and gym goers, parents of teen athletes, nurses and scrubs wearers. Awareness gap: no unaware or problem aware ads.
3. Angles: hidden cause (bacteria biofilm in synthetic fabric), failed solutions (vinegar, more detergent, hot wash), identity (runners), objection first (machine safety).
4. Concepts:
   - C021 runner identity: runner sniffs shirt after washing, "If your running shirts smell clean until you sweat, this is why" (unaware, UGC, outdoor).
   - C022 failed solutions listicle: "I tried vinegar, hot wash and sport detergent" static carousel (problem aware, carousel).
   - C023 mechanism demo: lab style split screen, biofilm explanation by a chemist (solution aware, expert demo) with consented, real expert.
   - C024 parent of teen athlete: hamper reveal POV, humor (problem aware, skit).
   - C025 machine safety objection: technician talking head (product aware, objection first).
5. Each changes persona, angle and format versus the live statics, so each passes the new concept test.
6. Test: 5 concepts x 2 executions in the testing lane, read after the minimum evidence rule (see testing module).
