# Copywriting Library

> Purpose: frameworks, formulas and platform rules for ad copy, statics and on-screen text. Every claim must come from the approved list in `BRAND.md` or have written substantiation. Write in customer language from the VOC bank.

## 1. Frameworks

### PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution)
Best for problem aware cold audiences.
```
Problem: Your gym shirts still smell after washing.
Agitate: You rewash, add more detergent, try vinegar, and the smell is back the minute you sweat.
Solution: [Product] breaks down the odor-causing buildup that regular detergent leaves in synthetic fabric.
CTA: Try it on your next load. 30 day money back.
```

### AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)
Best for longer primary text and landing page aligned copy.
```
Attention: 9 out of 10 runners we surveyed had this problem. (only if the survey is real)
Interest: Synthetic fabric traps odor in a way hot washes cannot reach.
Desire: Imagine pulling a shirt from the drawer that smells clean even mid run.
Action: Get the starter pack today.
```

### BAB (Before, After, Bridge)
Best for transformation and testimonial copy.
```
Before: Every long run ended with a shirt I could smell from across the room.
After: Now my kit smells clean even after a 20 miler.
Bridge: One capful of [Product] in the wash.
```

### 4Ps (Promise, Picture, Proof, Push)
Best for offers and product aware audiences.
```
Promise: Clean-smelling gym clothes, guaranteed.
Picture: Open your gym bag tomorrow and smell nothing but fresh laundry.
Proof: 3,400+ five star reviews. (real count, dated)
Push: Free shipping ends Sunday. (true)
```

### FAB (Features, Advantages, Benefits)
Best for B2B and technical products.
```
Feature: Auto-syncs with your accounting software.
Advantage: No manual exports at month end.
Benefit: Close the books two days faster.
```

### QUEST (Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition)
Best for long form, lead gen and B2B.

### 4Us for headlines
Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra specific. Score each headline 1 to 4 on each; keep those scoring 12+.

## 3. Objection handling matrix

Build from the VOC bank. Each objection gets a proof type and an ad concept.

| Objection (verbatim style) | Root anxiety | Answer | Proof format | Concept idea |
|---------------------------|--------------|--------|--------------|--------------|
| "Too expensive" | Value | Price per use, cost of the problem, comparison with alternatives | Calculator static, math video | "Let's do the math" objection first |
| "Will it work for me?" | Fit | Segment specific proof | Testimonials from that persona | Persona testimonial series |
| "I've tried everything" | Skepticism | New mechanism, why others failed | Demo, expert explainer | Failed solutions concept |
| "Is it safe?" | Risk | Certifications, ingredients, expert | Expert video, certification static | Objection first expert |
| "Hard to set up or use" | Effort | Speed demo | Screen recording, timer on screen | "Set up in 2 minutes" demo |
| "What if I don't like it?" | Regret | Guarantee, free returns | Policy card, founder promise | Risk reversal |
| "Never heard of you" | Trust | Social proof, press, founder | Review montage, founder story | Founder origin |
| "I'll do it later" | Inertia | Cost of waiting, true deadline | Countdown (true), seasonal | Cost of inaction |
| B2B: "We already use X" | Switching cost | Migration help, comparison | Comparison table, case study | Switch story |
| B2B: "Need approval" | Internal politics | ROI case, security docs | One page business case, document ad | Champion enablement |

## 4. Offer framing

**Value equation (Hormozi, $100M Offers):** perceived value rises with dream outcome and perceived likelihood of achievement, and falls with time delay and effort and sacrifice. Copy should raise the first two and shrink the last two.

| Lever | Copy move |
|-------|-----------|
| Dream outcome | Name the specific end state in the customer's words |
| Perceived likelihood | Proof: reviews, numbers, demos, guarantees |
| Time delay | "Results from the first wash", "live in a day" (only if true) |
| Effort and sacrifice | "One capful", "no setup", "we migrate for you" |

**Price framing:** per day or per use; versus the cost of the problem; versus an expensive alternative; bundle savings; anchor with the full price before the offer price (only real prices).

**Risk reversal:** money back guarantee, free returns, free trial, cancel anytime. State it in the ad when price or trust is the top objection.

**Urgency and scarcity:** only real deadlines and real stock limits. Fake countdowns are deceptive and against platform policies and consumer law in many markets.

**Discount vs value add:** test value adds (bonus, bundle, free shipping) against percent off; discounts train buyers and cut margin. Coordinate offer tests with `growth-orchestrator`.

## 5. Primary text guidance (Meta)

- First line is the hook; it must work alone (truncation at about 125 characters).
- Short (1 to 3 lines) for most aware and offer ads; long (storytelling, 5 to 15 lines) can work for founder stories and complex products. Test both as part of concept tests, not as the concept itself.
- Use line breaks, not walls of text. Emojis only if they fit the brand voice.
- Avoid second person statements implying personal attributes ("Are you depressed?", "Struggling with your debt?"). Meta's personal attributes policy rejects these. Write about the situation, not the person's attribute.
- Headline field: the benefit or offer, about 27 to 40 characters.

## 6. TikTok copy

- The caption is secondary; the hook lives in the video.
- Keep the caption short, conversational, and consistent with the on-screen hook.
- On-screen text follows TikTok native styles; avoid ad-like slogans.

## 7. LinkedIn copy

- Speak to a role and its metric: "Finance teams closing in 10+ days".
- First 150 characters carry the hook before "see more".
- Specific numbers and named outcomes (with permission) beat adjectives.
- Different angles for buying committee roles: user (ease), manager (team outcome), executive (cost, risk, revenue), IT or security (compliance).
- Document ads: title page states the promise; each page delivers one insight.

## 8. Google RSA writing system

Write headlines as independent units that combine in any order. Categories (aim for at least 2 to 3 per category across the 15 headlines):

| Category | Example (30 characters max) |
|----------|----------------------------|
| Keyword or intent match | Odor Remover for Gym Clothes |
| Primary benefit | Removes Odor Detergent Leaves |
| Proof | 3,400+ Five Star Reviews |
| Offer | Free Shipping Over $35 |
| Differentiator or mechanism | Breaks Down Odor Buildup |
| Objection killer | Safe for All Machines |
| Risk reversal | 30 Day Money Back Guarantee |
| CTA | Shop the Starter Pack Today |
| Brand | Official [Brand] Store |

Descriptions (90 characters): two benefit plus proof descriptions, one offer description, one objection or guarantee description.

Rules: avoid repeating the same phrase across headlines (Google needs variety); pin only for legal or brand requirements (pinning reduces combinations); check the asset report and replace low performers monthly; review AI generated or customized text before it serves. Hand implementation to `google-ads`.

## 9. ChatGPT and assistant ad copy

- Answer the question the user is asking in the conversation: lead with the specific fit ("For 5 person agencies: CRM set up in a day").
- Plain, factual, verifiable. Superlatives without proof look worse next to an AI answer.
- One intent per ad; map ad variants to intent clusters.
- Verify format and limits with `chatgpt-ads` before finalizing.

## 11. Copy QA checklist

- Uses VOC language; no generic adjectives (innovative, premium, best in class) without proof.
- Claims on the approved list; numbers sourced and dated.
- No personal attributes phrasing (Meta), no prohibited claims (health, finance) without approval.
- Character limits checked per platform.
- Spelling and brand names correct; localized copy reviewed by a native speaker.
- Offer, price and deadline match the landing page.
